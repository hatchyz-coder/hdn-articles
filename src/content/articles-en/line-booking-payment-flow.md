## Simply adding LINE does not create a connected patient journey

A clinic can open an official LINE account and still leave patients confused if booking, questionnaires, payment, and post-visit follow-up are handled in separate systems.

On the clinic side, staff often end up explaining each step individually, checking booking and payment status manually, and contacting patients one by one when an action is incomplete.

## Three common problems caused by fragmentation

### Patients drop off around the booking process

If a patient moves from LINE to a booking page and then has to jump repeatedly between different screens or services, every transition creates another opportunity to abandon the process.

### Staff spend more time checking status

When the clinic cannot see in one place who has booked, who has not completed the questionnaire, who is waiting for payment, and who has already received follow-up information, operational work becomes dependent on individual staff members.

### Follow-up after care is interrupted

If communication effectively ends after a single consultation or purchase, it becomes difficult to send appropriate reminders for follow-up visits or repeat purchases at the right time.

## Improvement should start with the sequence, not with adding another tool

The first step is to map the order in which patients receive information, make a booking, complete a questionnaire, and pay.

Looking at advertising, the website, LINE, booking, questionnaires, payment, and follow-up as one continuous flow makes it easier to find both the points where patients stop and the places where staff workload increases.

## Areas that can be organized with LHub

Depending on the clinic's workflow, LHub can support areas such as:

- Tags and labels based on patient status
- Different rich-menu displays for new patients, returning patients, and purchasers
- Navigation to questionnaires, booking, and payment
- Scenario-based messages after booking, consultation, or purchase
- Visibility into patients waiting for payment or due for follow-up

## Summary

The value of LINE is not simply the ability to send messages. The goal is to create a state in which patients can choose the next action without confusion and staff can operate the process consistently without excessive manual checking.

For clinics, patient journey design should therefore be evaluated as one connected operational system rather than as a set of separate digital tools.

> This article presents general principles for patient journey and operational design. Specific workflows should be adapted to each clinic's services, systems, and compliance requirements.
