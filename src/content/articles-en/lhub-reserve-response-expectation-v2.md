When an answer cannot be immediate, uncertainty often creates more friction than the wait itself. This guide focuses on response expectations and handoff design.

## Make the wait understandable

Fast replies are useful, but not every inquiry should be answered before the facts are checked. The better goal is to make the current status and next step understandable.

## Acknowledge, verify, then respond

Define what information is actually needed, who owns the check, and when the next update should happen. Do not promise a time the team cannot reliably meet.

## Separate urgent cases

Urgent, safety-related, or highly individual cases should not be processed like routine questions. Define a human escalation path before automating repetitive communication.

## Reduce repeat inquiries

Questions such as “Did you receive this?” or “When will I hear back?” often point to missing expectations in the first message. Use those repeated questions as design feedback.

## Do not assume undocumented product functions

This reserve article does not rely on an unverified LHub or LINE capability. Confirm current official product documentation before implementing any product-specific automation.

## Keep the human work human

Use automation for predictable steps and preserve human attention for exceptions, reassurance, and judgment. A good workflow reduces unnecessary repetition without making the experience feel closed or mechanical.
