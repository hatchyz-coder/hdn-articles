Japan's Consumer Affairs Agency publishes guidance on labeling, representations, and consumer-facing information. Those materials are useful for checking general principles such as clarity and avoidance of misleading claims.

Medical advertising is also subject to separate rules under healthcare law, so Consumer Affairs guidance should be treated as a supplemental reference rather than a substitute for medical-advertising requirements.

## Check whether claims can be substantiated

Statements about outcomes, superiority, pricing, or patient benefits should be supported by appropriate evidence and presented with the conditions needed to avoid misunderstanding.

## Make prices and conditions clear

Clinics offering private services should make it easy for patients to understand the price, what is included, and whether additional costs may arise.

## Use photos and testimonials carefully

Before-and-after images and patient testimonials can create a strong impression of expected results. Consent, privacy, individual variation, and the applicable medical-advertising rules all need to be considered.

## Review every channel

Website pages, social posts, printed materials, in-clinic displays, and staff explanations should not contradict one another.

## Practical takeaway

A useful compliance review asks two questions: is the statement legally permitted, and could an ordinary patient reasonably misunderstand it? Both perspectives matter.
