The value of a traditional Japanese home cannot be communicated through a floor plan and price sheet alone. Heavy timber beams, aged fittings, natural light, and the character of the surrounding community often become tangible only during an on-site visit.

For renovation businesses, however, the customer journey is long. An inquiry may lead to a tour, financing questions, design work, construction, and months of post-contract coordination. When inquiries arrive by email, bookings by phone, documents as PDFs, and project updates through personal messaging accounts, both customers and staff lose track of the next step.

LINE should therefore be designed not merely as a promotional channel, but as a continuous customer pathway. LHub can connect the information and operational steps behind that pathway.

## A record number of vacant homes does not automatically create consultations

Japan's Ministry of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism reports that the 2023 Housing and Land Survey recorded 9.002 million vacant homes, representing a record vacancy rate of 13.8%. The government also supports nationwide vacant-home and vacant-land banks to encourage reuse and circulation.

Yet making property information visible does not remove the uncertainty surrounding renovation. Prospective customers may need to understand seismic performance, insulation, water damage, access requirements, change-of-use rules, subsidies, and the wide range of possible construction costs. Many are interested but do not know which question to ask first.

The first thing a renovation business needs to offer is not a construction contract. It is a safe and understandable way to begin asking questions.

## Four touchpoints to connect with LHub

### 1. Turn document requests into useful context

After a customer adds the company's LINE account, use a short form to identify the current stage:

- searching for a property;
- considering how to use an owned property;
- planning accommodation or retail use;
- researching renovation costs or subsidies.

LHub can organize these answers as customer data and tags. A person who is still searching should not receive the same message as an owner ready for an inspection. Simple classification at the entrance makes later conversations more relevant.

### 2. Reduce uncertainty before the property tour

A booking confirmation should include more than the date and time. Send the meeting point, parking information, expected duration, recommended clothing, rain policy, and accessibility notes in advance.

LINE has 100 million monthly active users in Japan as of March 2026, making it a familiar communication channel. Connecting booking and customer information in LHub helps the operator send practical instructions only to the people who need them.

### 3. Present a clear next step within 24 hours

Interest is concrete immediately after a tour, but individual follow-up can be delayed on busy days. A tour-completion trigger can present several options:

- request an initial cost consultation;
- arrange an inspection of an owned property;
- discuss financing or subsidy research;
- discuss commercial use such as lodging or retail;
- continue gathering information without a sales call.

Not every visitor should be pushed into an immediate sales meeting. Giving customers permission to wait preserves trust and keeps the relationship open.

### 4. Keep the contact point consistent after the contract

Traditional-home renovation often reveals additional work after demolition begins. Project photos, specification approvals, meeting bookings, and updated estimates increase communication after the contract.

If the sales channel and project channel are completely separate, customers may not know whom to contact. LHub can help staff track the assigned person, project stage, and pending confirmation while keeping the customer-facing entrance consistent.

## Some decisions should not be automated

Traditional homes vary significantly. Structural safety, subsidy eligibility, final cost, and schedule should never be guaranteed before an appropriate inspection.

Automation is well suited to confirmations, preparation lists, standard process explanations, and staff handoffs. Professional judgment and property-specific commitments must remain with qualified people.

Addresses, plans, ownership status, and financing details also require clear collection purposes, access controls, and retention rules. Sensitive project information should not remain only in a chat history.

## Metrics for the first 30 days

Do not judge the system only by revenue in its first month. Measure where the pathway is blocked:

- completion rate of the stage-identification form;
- conversion from form completion to tour booking;
- tour attendance and cancellation rates;
- next-action selection within 24 hours of a tour;
- first-response time;
- unresolved inquiries by staff member.

Follower growth alone does not prove that the process works. Even with a smaller audience, knowing where each customer stopped makes improvement possible.

## Final take

LHub's role in traditional-home renovation is not simply to make old buildings look attractive in promotional messages. It is to connect document requests, tour bookings, consultations, and post-contract communication so customers do not become lost during a long decision process.

Preserving a building for the next generation requires patient, stage-appropriate communication. Connecting useful information with timely human support is the operational foundation for that work.
